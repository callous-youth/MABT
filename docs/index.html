Overview

MABT anchors adversarial trajectories to data manifolds to improve transfer across models.

MABT combines manifold anchoring with distributional bilevel optimization. The manifold anchoring projector (MAP) rectifies attack states, while the bilevel formulation learns an initialization under local surrogate uncertainty. Together, they guide the subsequent attack toward directions that transfer beyond a single surrogate.

Method

Anchor the trajectory. Learn the initialization.

MABT integrates geometric trajectory guidance and transfer-aware initialization in a practical two-stage procedure.

01 · Manifold anchoring projector

Keep the trajectory aligned

MAP balances fidelity to the current attack state with a manifold prior, while the feasible set retains the perturbation constraint. This is the ideal variational formulation; practical operators such as Resize provide efficient approximations to the anchoring effect.

02 · Distributional bilevel optimization

Learn a transferable initialization

The lower level produces an anchored perturbation from the initialization; the upper level selects an initialization by evaluating the resulting perturbation under a distribution of surrogate variants.

The distributional risk is evaluated on the unanchored input x + δ, consistent with the camera-ready formulation:

03 · Hessian-free solver

Optimize with first-order gradients

A one-step probe approximates the gap correction, avoiding Hessian-vector products. The resulting initialization is used by the final manifold-anchored attack.

Analysis

Geometry, transfer and efficiency.

We examine the transfer behavior of the learned trajectory through loss landscapes, victim-side feature shifts, and computational trade-offs.

What do MAP and DBO contribute?

Both components improve the PGD baseline individually; combining them yields the highest average ASR for each of the four model-related configurations shown.

How do practical MAP choices compare?

The historical full MAP ablation compares explicit, discriminative, and generative realizations under PGD. Resize gives a low-cost choice; diffusion-based realizations achieve higher ASR at higher computational cost.
