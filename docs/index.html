Geometric perspective

Transfer fails when ambient-space attack updates drift toward surrogate-specific, off-manifold directions.

Our starting point is not a stronger gradient heuristic, but a geometric disconnect: iterative updates can accumulate components that are effective on one surrogate yet poorly shared by unseen victims. MABT addresses this mismatch at two coupled levels. MAP rectifies each intermediate attack state toward a manifold-compatible region; DBO selects the attack trajectory under a local distribution of surrogate variations, so that its final response is assessed beyond one deterministic decision boundary.

Method

Rectify geometric drift, then optimize for transfer under uncertainty.

MABT joins a state-level geometric correction with a distributional, finite-step bilevel objective—then solves the resulting hierarchy with only first-order quantities.

01 · Manifold Anchoring Projector

Rectify off-manifold attack states

Given an ambient adversarial state \(\mathbf z=\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\delta\), MAP defines its ideal anchoring target as a fidelity–geometry trade-off inside the same perturbation set. Practical realizations—such as Resize, bit-depth reduction, denoising, or generative purification—instantiate this operator with different anchoring–efficiency trade-offs.

\[\mathcal P_{\mathcal M}[\mathbf z]:=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf u\in\mathbf x+\Omega}\Big(\underbrace{\Psi(\mathbf u,\mathbf z)}_{\text{fidelity to current state}}+\underbrace{\mathcal R_{\mathcal M}(\mathbf u)}_{\text{manifold-compatible prior}}\Big).\]

MAP is inserted before surrogate evaluation, so the attack is optimized on a rectified state while the \(\ell_p\) perturbation budget remains explicit.

02 · Distributional Bilevel Optimization

Optimize a geometry-aligned trajectory under surrogate uncertainty

DBO couples the MAP-conditioned lower-level response with an upper-level transfer-risk criterion. The lower level generates a perturbation from \(\boldsymbol\xi\); the upper level evaluates that induced response over a local family of surrogate variants. Thus, \(\boldsymbol\xi\) is not merely an initialization to tune—it parameterizes the starting state of a trajectory selected for its distributional transfer behavior.

\[\min_{\boldsymbol\xi\in\Omega}\;F(\boldsymbol\xi,\boldsymbol\delta^*(\boldsymbol\xi)):=-\mathcal R_{\mathrm{dist}}(\boldsymbol\delta^*(\boldsymbol\xi);\pi),\]
\[\text{s.t.}\quad\boldsymbol\delta^*(\boldsymbol\xi)=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\boldsymbol\delta\in\Omega}-\mathcal L\!\left(\mathcal S\!\left(\mathcal P_{\mathcal M}[\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\delta]\right),\mathbf y\right).\]

with \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{dist}}(\boldsymbol\delta;\pi)=\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\theta\sim\pi}[\mathcal L(\mathcal S_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\delta),\mathbf y)]\), which evaluates the final response over local surrogate variations.

03 · Hessian-free solution strategy

Probe, correct, and update with first-order quantities

Rather than differentiating through the lower-level trajectory, MABT probes a nearby response, estimates the regularized lower-level gap, and combines this correction with the distributional-risk gradient. This yields a practical first-order meta update with no Hessian–vector products.

Probe\[\hat{\boldsymbol\delta}=\Pi_\Omega\!\left(\boldsymbol\delta^*+\sigma\nabla_{\boldsymbol\delta}\mathcal L(\boldsymbol\delta^*)\right)\]
Gap correction\[\nabla\mathcal G\approx\nabla_{\boldsymbol\delta}\mathcal L(\hat{\boldsymbol\delta})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol\delta}\mathcal L(\boldsymbol\delta^*)\]
Meta update\[\nabla_{\boldsymbol\xi}\phi\approx\lambda^{-1}\nabla_{\boldsymbol\delta}\mathcal R_{\mathrm{dist}}-\nabla\mathcal G\]

Two stages, one coherent trajectory

Stage I uses the inner response, gap probe, and distributional risk to refine the trajectory’s starting state. Stage II resets at that state and performs the final MAP-conditioned attack with the selected base attacker.

The solver preserves the attack routine while injecting geometric rectification and transfer-aware meta guidance.
